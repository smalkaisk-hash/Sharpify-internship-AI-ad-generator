# Anti-AI Slop Rules (MANDATORY for ALL Writing)

> Based on Leila Hormozi's internal memo on AI-written content.
> These rules apply to EVERY piece of written output — ads, emails, funnels, offers, content, Slack messages, docs. No exceptions.

---

## Banned Phrases (Never Use These)

### Dead Giveaway AI Words
- "Delve into" / "Unpack" — say "look at" or "break down"
- "This signals that" / "This underscores" — say what's actually happening
- "Navigate the complexities of" — say what the actual complexity is
- "In an ever-changing landscape" — say what's actually changing
- "Synergies" / "Leverage our learnings" / "Holistic approach" — say "here's what worked, here's what didn't, here's what we're changing"
- "Harness" / "Harnessing" — just say "use"
- "Elevate" / "Elevating" — say "improve" or be specific
- "Streamline" — say what you're actually making faster/simpler
- "Empower" / "Empowering" — say what the person can now do
- "Robust" — say what makes it strong
- "Seamless" / "Seamlessly" — say what makes it easy
- "Cutting-edge" / "State-of-the-art" — say what's new about it
- "Game-changer" / "Game-changing" — say what it actually changes
- "Revolutionize" — say what's different now
- "Unlock" (as in "unlock potential") — say what they get
- "Foster" (as in "foster a culture of") — say what you're building and how
- "Landscape" (as in "competitive landscape") — say "market" or be specific
- "Resonate" / "Resonates with" — say "matters to" or "connects with"
- "Ecosystem" (when not about nature) — say what you actually mean

### Banned Transition Words (at start of paragraphs)
- "Moreover"
- "Furthermore"
- "That said"
- "Additionally"
- "Importantly"
- "It's worth noting"
- "It's important to note"
- "In conclusion"
- "Ultimately"
- "At the end of the day"

Just start the next sentence. If the connection isn't obvious, the writing needs restructuring — not a transition word.

### Banned Structures
- **"Bold word: colon: explanation" bullet lists** — the Clarity/Alignment/Execution pattern. If you need bullets, write real sentences with real information.
- **Forced negation** — "Not this, but that" / "Instead of X, Y" as a rhetorical pattern. Say what you mean directly.
- **Staccato repetitions** — "Not this, or this, or this, but that." Just state the point.
- **Excessive adverbs** — "quietly underscores", "fundamentally reshapes", "truly transforms". Drop the adverb. Say "this is important" not "this quietly underscores."

## The 5 AI Tells to Avoid

### 1. The Corporate Therapist Voice
Bad: "This is a powerful opportunity to lean into our strengths and foster a culture of accountability."
Good: "We're good at X. We need to hold each other to deadlines. Here's how."

### 2. The Neat Little Bow
Bad: "Ultimately, the goal is to build a more resilient and agile organization."
Good: Skip it. If your conclusion could apply to every company on earth, it's not a conclusion. It's filler.

### 3. The "Says Everything, Means Nothing" Paragraph
Test: Read the paragraph. Can you summarize what it actually said in plain English? If not — delete it. No writing is better than empty writing.

### 4. Connecting Ideas Without an Opinion
Bad: "This signals a shift in customer behavior and underscores the need for a revised approach."
Good: "Customers are doing X, so we need to change Y." — Have an actual opinion. State the cause and the action.

### 5. Vague Complexity Claims
Bad: "As we navigate the complexities of scaling in an ever-changing landscape..."
Good: "Scaling is hard because [specific reason]. Here's what we're doing about it."

## What to Do Instead

### Write Like a Human
- Read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it in conversation, don't write it.
- Use the words a real person would use — "look at" not "delve into", "use" not "leverage", "help" not "empower"
- Short sentences. Plain words. Say what you mean.
- Have an actual opinion — don't just connect two ideas and call it analysis

### The Paragraph Test
Every paragraph must pass this: can you tell someone the point of this paragraph in one plain sentence? If not, rewrite or delete.

### Be Specific, Not Impressive
- Bad: "We've seen significant improvements across multiple key metrics"
- Good: "Close rate went from 10% to 16%. Show rate went from 49% to 56%."

### Cut the Wrapping Paper
Don't pad your real point with filler. If you have 3 things to say, say 3 things. Don't wrap each one in a paragraph of context.

## Quick Self-Check Before Delivering Any Copy

1. Does it sound like a human wrote it? (Read it out loud)
2. Could this apply to literally any business? → Too generic, rewrite
3. Are there banned phrases? → Find and replace with plain language
4. Does every paragraph have a clear point? → If not, cut it
5. Are there "impressive-sounding" sentences that say nothing? → Delete them
6. Does it start paragraphs with transition words? → Remove them
7. Is there a neat bow at the end that means nothing? → Cut it or make it specific
